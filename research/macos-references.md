# Mac apps: practice, criticism and literature behind the macOS presets

Gathered 2026-10-21 for the three Mac-only presets (`native-craft`, `pro-instrument`, `document-first`) and the `mac` blocks in `library/directions.json`. ✅ = read at the source; ◐ = from secondary coverage of the source; 🛠 = my synthesis, not a single cited claim.

## 1. What Mac users and Mac developers say a Mac app is

- **"Mac-assed Mac app."** Brent Simmons popularised the phrase in 2020 (from Collin Donnell) for software that is unapologetically a Mac app: platform-specific, using the platform's conventions instead of a flashy custom UI, which often costs accessibility too. His example is NetNewsWire; his counter-example is Slack. He prefers it to "native", since an app can be technically native and still feel foreign. ◐ [Daring Fireball, 2020](https://daringfireball.net/linked/2020/03/20/mac-assed-mac-apps). Alan Jacobs names Transmit as the most Mac-assed app ◐ [ayjay.org](https://social.ayjay.org/2023/09/12/i-often-think.html).
- **Stock is not the same as Mac-like (2026).** Simmons now argues that the three reasons to use stock Mac UI fail. Users don't care whether UI is stock: they learn Things' custom UI without trouble. Stock wasn't cheaper for NetNewsWire: it needed more Liquid Glass work than Things' custom UI. And Apple's current direction is off target, though not its designers. His experiment removed the NSToolbar and full-height sidebar, wanted colour and spacing back in toolbar icons, and pointed to Things: a toolbar not at the top of the window, no translucency in the main UI, content that doesn't slide under the sidebar. ✅ [inessential.com, 2026-09-22](https://inessential.com/2026/09/22/that-about-wraps-it-up-for.html). Gruber agrees that the stock Mac UI is in a bad place and that "Mac-like" has many meanings ✅ [Daring Fireball, 2026-09-25](https://daringfireball.net/linked/2026/09/25/brent-simmons-stock-mac-ui).
  - 🛠 Consequence for the skill: "native" has two honest forms. *Quiet native* uses the system's controls and puts the brand in one place. *Owned chrome* draws its own window but keeps every Mac behaviour (menus, shortcuts, focus, drag and drop, right-click, a draggable title area). Both are dealt; a skin that drops Mac behaviour is neither.
- **What breaks the feel.** Users of non-native apps name concrete things: a preferences window that isn't one, button placement, menus that can't leave the window, scrolling without Mac inertia or rubber-banding, weak feedback when a window loses focus. ◐ [1Password community](https://www.1password.community/1password-at-home-31/1password-8-non-native-feel-16936/index10.html). Gruber's long-running case against Electron is about exactly these. ✅ [Daring Fireball, 2018](https://daringfireball.net/2018/12/electron_and_the_decline_of_native_apps).
- **Panic's Nova as the model of balance.** MacStories credits Nova's Mac-likeness to a Settings window modelled on System Settings whose changes apply immediately, a Files sidebar that behaves like Finder, standard shortcuts and right-click menus, and a thin clickable status bar. The brand gets one lavish moment, an animated "lightspeed" welcome that follows the pointer; everything after it is convention. ✅ [MacStories review](https://www.macstories.net/reviews/nova-review-panics-code-editor-demonstrates-why-mac-like-design-matters/).
- **Ivory for Mac.** Up to six user-arranged columns (one extra on iPad). ✅ [MacStories](https://www.macstories.net/reviews/ivory-for-mac-review-tapbots-superb-mastodon-client-comes-to-apple-desktops-and-laptops/). A design breakdown describes system fonts throughout, with the brand carried by mascot art, sound and selectable accent themes ◐ [blakecrosley.com](https://blakecrosley.com/guides/design/ivory).
- **Things 3.** Mostly white space, with bold type, custom icons and thoughtful splashes of colour; Magic Plus as the one draggable creation affordance. ✅ [MacStories review](https://www.macstories.net/reviews/things-3-beauty-and-delight-in-a-task-manager/).

## 2. Apple's own guidance, and where it went wrong

- **HIG, Designing for macOS.** Use large displays to show more content in fewer nested levels with less modality, at a comfortable density. Let people resize, hide and move windows, and support full screen. Put every command in the menu bar. Support high-precision pointing and keyboard shortcuts, including keyboard-only work styles. Support personalisation: toolbars, which views show, and colours and fonts. Sessions run from minutes to hours, often with several apps open. ✅ [HIG](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/designing-for-macos).
- **Tahoe (macOS 26) criticism.** Floating translucent sidebars made it hard to tell a sidebar from a window behind it. Toolbars were grey platters on grey. Translucent controls were hard to read over scrolled content. MacRumors found the glass "cobbled together" with the older language. ◐ [MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2025/08/01/macos-tahoe-review), [bzamayo](https://bzamayo.com/tahoe-toolbars), [Eclectic Light](https://eclecticlight.co/2025/12/28/last-year-on-my-mac-look-back-in-disbelief/).
- **Menu icons.** Tahoe put an icon on almost every menu item. Nikita Prokopov (tonsky.me) showed there aren't enough good metaphors for that: more than ten icons for "New" across apps, the same icon for different actions, all at about 12×12 pt. The 1992 Macintosh HIG had warned against it, and Apple's current guidance now says to use menu icons sparingly. ◐ [Pixel Envy](https://pxlnv.com/linklog/tahoe-icons-inconsistent-confusing-illegible/), [Macworld](https://www.macworld.com/article/3025010/macos-tahoes-icons-are-a-mess-but-not-the-ones-you-think.html).
- **macOS 27 "Golden Gate" corrections (Sept 2026).** Sidebars are edge to edge again with no shadow, and sidebar icons are coloured again. Corner radii are uniform and smaller, toolbars are uniform across apps, and active windows are easier to tell from inactive ones. A Liquid Glass slider in Appearance goes from clear to more opaque. ✅ [MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/06/09/macos-golden-gate-liquid-glass/). MacStories calls this a practical usability win and notes menu bar crowding on notched MacBooks, now helped by an overflow control. ✅ [MacStories macOS 27 review](https://www.macstories.net/stories/macos-27-the-macstories-review/3/).
  - 🛠 Consequence: check text over materials at the user's chosen glass setting, not only at the default. Keep sidebars edge to edge. Don't put an icon on every menu item. Keep menu bar extras short (≤6 characters of live text) so the notch doesn't hide them.

## 3. Pro and keyboard-first practice

- **Raycast** states three design principles: fast, simple, delightful. Its redesign brought a larger search bar and icons, an action bar for the selection's functions, a Compact Mode, and a keycap app icon that signals keyboard use. ✅ [Raycast blog](https://www.raycast.com/blog/a-fresh-look-and-feel).
- **The "Linear style" is now a tell.** Dark backgrounds, blurred gradient art and glowing accents on every SaaS page. Critics describe it as monotonous and forgettable, and say AI tools reproduce it by default. Linear's own *app* is keyboard-first and dense, which is the part worth borrowing. ◐ [LogRocket](https://blog.logrocket.com/ux-design/linear-design), [Design This Week](https://designthisweek.substack.com/p/twid-linear-style-is-now-illegal), [uxdesign.cc](https://uxdesign.cc/the-problem-with-best-practices-in-the-age-of-ai-642987dc9ec9).
- **Pro creative apps** (Logic Pro, Final Cut Pro, Pixelmator Pro) put the content in the centre with tools and a tabbed inspector at the edges, on neutral dark grey chrome. 🛠 By observation; no design-team rationale was found. The usual argument is that neutral chrome keeps the content's colour judgement honest, and a photo editor's founder makes it explicitly ◐ [blakecrosley.com on Darkroom](https://blakecrosley.com/de/guides/design/darkroom).
- **Density follows expertise.** Experts chunk domain patterns and handle denser screens, and design systems recommend higher density, user-adjustable, for information-heavy expert tasks. ◐ [Basis design system](https://design.basis.com/foundations/density). The HIG asks for a "comfortable" density, which on a Mac is far higher than on a phone (§2).

## 4. Literature

- **Fitts on the menu bar.** The menu bar sits on the screen edge, so the pointer stops there however hard it is flung: an effectively infinite-height target, Tognazzini's "mile-high menu bar". Corners are stronger still. His caveat: the benefit presupposes good pointer acceleration, and it weakens on trackpads. ◐ [NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/fitts-law/), [TidBITS 1992](https://tidbits.com/1992/06/15/menu-usage/), [Coding Horror](https://blog.codinghorror.com/fitts-law-and-infinite-width/). It supports a menu bar extra as the cheapest glance on a Mac.
- **Hotkeys.** Hotkeys make experts much faster but are learned slowly, and modest changes to menu feedback and cost speed up learning significantly. Showing and voicing the mapping helps, and so does making the menu route costlier. ✅ [Grossman, Dragicevic & Balakrishnan, CHI 2007](https://www.dgp.toronto.edu/~ravin/papers/chi2007_learninghotkeys.pdf). Experienced users still rarely adopt shortcuts unprompted ◐ (Lane et al. 2005, via secondary sources).
- **Novice to expert.** Interfaces must prompt the switch to expert methods, minimise the performance dip, and allow a high ceiling (Scarr, Cockburn, Gutwin & Quinn, CHI 2011, "Dips and ceilings"). The survey is Cockburn et al., *ACM Computing Surveys* 2014, doi:10.1145/2659796. ◐ Lowering error rates alone doesn't make people switch (Goguey et al. 2019) ◐.
  - 🛠 Consequence: show the shortcut where the click happens (a keycap on the row and in the command palette, not only in the menu), and keep every command in the menu bar, which is the discoverability layer.

## 5. Writing and document apps

- **iA Writer.** One of iA's goals was a writing app without settings. Focus Mode dims everything but the current sentence, the Mac top bar disappears while you type, and Markdown lets you keep your hands off the mouse. ✅ [iA, iA Writer for Mac](https://ia.net/topics/ia-writer-for-mac). Its faces (iA Writer Mono/Duo/Quattro) are open source under the OFL, derived from IBM Plex Mono.
- **Ulysses and Bear** (🛠 by use): a library sidebar, a sheet list showing first lines, and an editor that styles the markup instead of hiding it. The panes collapse one at a time.
